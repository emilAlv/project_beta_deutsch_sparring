## How levels are assigned

This is a **working instructional sequence**, not a claim that a particular German grammatical structure belongs exclusively to a single CEFR level. A1 may introduce a fixed phrase, and A2 can require productive use with multiple cases or past-time contexts. A1 and A2 official competency descriptions describe communicative ability, not a fixed grammar table. The Goethe A1 course includes `seit` with dative, personal pronouns in the dative, basic `Perfekt` and some location/direction phrases. Its A2 course also returns to temporal prepositions in longer narratives.

**Codes:** **A1** = core or late A1 use; **bridge** = receptive A1 / productive A2; **A2** = systematic productive contrast / interaction; **B1-later** = explicitly defer for now. Some handwritten notes have mixed difficulty and are therefore cross-indexed.

## Recommended A1 scope from this album

- **A1 time:** `wann`, day/date/month/clock time, `am/um/im`, `von … bis`, `ab`, basic `seit wann/wie lange` with predictable answers, `in zwei Wochen` as a practical future reference.
- **A1 classroom and daily language:** polite instructions and du/ihr/Sie imperative; `bitte`, `doch`, `mal` as pragmatic softeners; basic `sollen`; simple possession and body-part descriptions.
- **A1 town and travel:** ask where a place is, understand `geradeaus/links/rechts`, `zum/zur/mit dem`, simple transport questions, simple `wo/wohin` meaning with memorised destinations.
- **A1 shopping:** price, basic preferences and basic `dieser/welcher` expressions as fixed questions. Memorised `Das gefällt mir` is possible without a full dative-verb paradigm.

## Recommended A2 scope from this album

- **A2 temporal consolidation:** `seit` (continuing) vs `vor` (ago) vs `nach` (after) vs `in` (time until an event); dative endings, a duration versus a start date, and switching between plans, ongoing situations and finished events.
- **A2 cases:** systematic dative personal pronouns and verb valency (e.g. `helfen`, `gefallen`, `passen`); distinguishing `Ich sehe ihn` from `Ich helfe ihm` and `Ich gebe ihm ein Buch`.
- **A2 movement versus position:** `wo?` + dative and `wohin?` + accusative with two-way prepositions; a location versus placing or moving something. These are two-way-preposition patterns, not a universal rule for all `wohin` expressions: `Ich gehe zum Arzt` still uses dative.
- **A2 deeper lexical control:** productive `welcher/dieser` across cases; reflexive pronouns; `lassen` in simple scenarios; selected directions `rein/raus, hinein/heraus`.
- **Later than A2 default:** subordinate temporal conjunction `bevor`, especially extended subordinate-clause chains, should not be mixed into the beginner `vor` preposition activity. It may be introduced receptively earlier if the class already knows it.

## Language validation notes and traps

- `seit` can be introduced as **A1 recognition and formulaic production**, even though systematic dative inflection and contrasting timelines are better rehearsed at A2.
- `vor einem Monat` (ago) is not the same as `vor dem Unterricht` (before class), and `bevor` introduces a subordinate clause: `Bevor der Kurs beginnt, …`. Do not collapse these structures into one flashcard.
- `in zwei Stunden` answers **when in the future?**, whereas `zwei Stunden lang` or `zwei Stunden` answers **how long?**.
- `bei + Dativ` indicates location near/with a person in the examples. `Ich gehe zum Arzt` is not a two-way-preposition accusative exception; `zu` always takes dative.
- With two-way prepositions, the decision is about **location vs directional change**, not whether a verb literally contains movement: `Ich laufe im Park` (running in the park) differs from `Ich laufe in den Park` (running into the park).
- `gefallen` grammar: `Die Jacke gefällt mir.` / `Die Jacken gefallen mir.`; in a simple A1 class, `Das gefällt mir` may be memorised, while A2 should explain the dative experiencer and subject agreement.
- `passen`, `helfen`, `gefallen`, `schmecken`, `gehören` are useful dative-verb examples; **`fragen` generally takes an accusative person** and `erklären` commonly uses a dative recipient plus accusative topic.
- Use **Swiss Standard German** in authored examples: `Velo`, `Fuss`, `Grüsse`, no `ß`. Provide alternative German Standard forms when valid; distinguish informal `rein/raus` from `hinein/hinaus`.
